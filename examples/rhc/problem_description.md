Problem: Effect of Right Heart Catheterization on 
30-Day Mortality in Critically Ill Patients

Research Question: What is the causal effect of right heart catheterization (RHC) 
administered on the first day of ICU admission on 30-day mortality in critically 
ill patients, and which patient subgroups benefit from or are harmed by RHC?

Causal Relationship: RHC is an invasive hemodynamic monitoring procedure administered 
at physician discretion based on clinical judgment and patient severity. Despite 
widespread adoption, the causal effect of RHC on survival remains contested. The 
seminal observational study by Connors et al. (1996) found RHC associated with 
increased mortality after propensity score adjustment, challenging the assumption 
that aggressive hemodynamic monitoring improves outcomes. This analysis estimates 
the population-level causal effect of RHC after controlling for observed confounders, 
with policy recommendations targeting physician-level patient selection criteria.

Causal mechanisms include:
* RHC provides hemodynamic data enabling targeted fluid and vasopressor management
* Procedural complications (arrhythmia, thrombosis, infection) may increase mortality
* Confounding by indication: physicians selectively administer RHC to more severely 
  ill patients, requiring careful confounder adjustment
* Information from RHC may trigger more aggressive interventions with uncertain 
  net effect on mortality
